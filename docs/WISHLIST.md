# PSDR — Wishlist & Roadmap

_Captured 2026-11-02 on branch `fix/hmc1023-lpf`. A living list — edit freely. Nothing below is committed to beyond the "Now" section._

**The vision:** make the PSDR a genuinely functional, pocketable two-way HF radio. Keep what the PSDR already does well — big color LCD, Cortex-M7 DSP headroom, waterfall, ergonomics, case, VNA/antenna analyzer, GPS — and fold in what the uSDX / (t)rueSDX does well: an efficient switching PA and real output filtering.

## Now — active work (this branch)
- **Boot fixes (issue #19):** remove the dead debug `while(1)` before the main loop (`main.c:792`); bound the Si5338 I²C wait so a missing/stuck synth shows a visible fault instead of a silent hang.
- **HMC1023 LPF/VGA bring-up:** blue-wire VDDBG→5 V (done on the bench); firmware to enable `LPF_POWER` (PE11), SPI-configure the chip, and flip the mux (PE10/PD8) from bypass into the filter path. Adds gain + selectivity.
- **Chase the real deafness:** the mux parks on **bypass**, so the dead filter is NOT the cause — suspect the QSD/mixer front end (the mux→mixer change) and/or the RF amp (HMC482 / `AMP_POWER`) path. Hardware review to follow.

## Firmware-only (works on the current hardware)
- **GPS: talking (2026-11-10).** `Source/src/gps.c` receives NMEA from U185 on USART6 (PC6/PC7, 9600 baud) and parses time, date, fix, satellites and position from RMC/GGA; console `N` reports it (`Np0`/`Np1` power, `Nr` reset). Verified indoors: sentences arrive with good checksums, no fix. Findings: the module's power pin (PA8) is active-low and it has been powered from boot all along (now switched off in sleep); the reset line is PD15, not PE15 as hal.c had it. Still to do: get a fix outdoors, set the RTC from GPS time, show position/satellites on screen, and use the 1PPS line (PE7) to **discipline the reference** / auto-fill the frequency calibration.
- **SD card: readable (2026-11-10).** `Source/src/sdcard.c` uses the MCU's native SD interface (SDMMC1, 4-bit pins PC8-PC12 + PD2, card detect PD13). Console `S` identifies the card and reads sector 0, read-only. Verified with an 8 GB card: 7639 MB, one partition, exFAT. Next: add a FAT file system (FatFs). Cards of 32 GB and under are simplest reformatted as FAT32; exFAT (how 64 GB+ cards and this 8 GB one are formatted) needs FatFs's exFAT option. Then: settings and frequency memories in a file, the band/station list for waterfall overlays, and recording / playing audio or raw I/Q (48 kHz 16-bit mono is about 345 MB per hour, stereo I/Q about 690 MB per hour). The SD interface's DMA would share DMA2 stream 3 with the display, so keep SD on polling or move one of them.
- **Waterfall: steady scroll rate, DONE 2026-11-10.** It used to add a line per main-loop pass, so it sped up in transmit and between CW elements, and FFTs between lines were dropped (the old "flicker"). Now every FFT goes into a per-bin peak hold and a line is drawn every 50 ms. Michael may want different transmit colours later.
- **VNA: first readings (2026-11-10).** The AD8302's outputs reach the MCU: MAGNITUDE on PB0 (ADC channel 8) and PHASE on PC5 (ADC channel 15). Console `h` reads them; `hb1` flips the impedance-bridge switch and `hx1` the RF-port switch while transmitting. Measured with the antenna port on a 50 ohm load (pad + analyzer), transmitting through the HMC1023:

  | Bridge | Port | At the antenna port | AD8302 magnitude | Phase output |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 0 | 0 | -1 dBm | +20 to +22 dB | 1.4 V |
  | 1 | 0 | -7 dBm (6.5 dB less) | **+5.9 to +6.5 dB** | 2.0 V (pinned at its 0 degree end) |
  | 0 | 1 | -35 dBm | +52 dB (pinned) | |
  | 1 | 1 | -25 to -35 dBm | +52 dB (pinned) | |

  So `IMP_BRIDGE_SWITCH_A=1/B=0` inserts the bridge (the 6.5 dB drop), and `TX_RF_SWITCH_A=0/B=1` sends the signal to the other connector. With the bridge in, the detector reads the ratio of the reference node to the antenna node: a 50 ohm load against the bridge's 50 ohm arm should give exactly 6.0 dB, and it reads 5.9 to 6.5 dB from 7 to 28.5 MHz. That is the antenna-analyzer measurement working in principle. Not done: any load other than 50 ohms (need an open, a short and a few known resistors/capacitors to check it), turning the two voltages into impedance / SWR (|Vant/Vref| = |Z/(Z+50)|; the phase output has no sign and is unreliable near 0 and 180 degrees), a frequency sweep and a plot, and the through measurement on the second port. These states still need to go into `signalPath()` properly (`vna_reflected`, `vna_through`).
- **CW transmit: first version DONE 2026-11-10.** `Source/src/cw.c`: console `q<wpm> <text>` sends text as Morse (a beacon in one line), and `qk1` turns the paddles into an iambic keyer (thumb = dit, index = dah; the squeeze-to-talk PTT is off while it is on). Timing runs from the 1 ms tick; the keyed signal is a 700 Hz tone through the sideband filter with a ~8 ms shaped rise. Measured on the analyzer at 20 and 25 wpm: dits and dahs exact (60/180 ms, 48/144 ms), key clicks -40 dBc at 100 Hz and below -50 dBc beyond 250 Hz. To do: the paddle keyer is untested with real paddles; sidetone (the DACs are busy driving the transmitter); carrier leak sits only about 24 dB below the keyed signal with the chip gain on, so a transmit DC-offset trim is needed; a CW receive mode (narrow filter, mode label); put speed and keyer on/off in the menu.
- **Smart CW (Morse) decoder:** Goertzel / narrow band-pass + adaptive threshold + WPM tracking; decoded text under the waterfall.

  - **Receive works across the whole tuning range, 2.5 to 175 MHz**, including 2 m. In bypass it is flat; through the chip it loses 4 to 10 dB above 100 MHz.
  - **The HMC1023's +20 dB improves sensitivity by about 22 dB**, i.e. by its whole gain. On a board with the VDDBG fix, receive should be run on "+20 dB".
  - **Root cause of the deafness: the first baseband amplifier is noisy, so gain is needed ahead of it.** (This corrects a first reading of the same data that blamed the ADC alone.) The baseband in-amps U255/U272 are MCP6N11-010 (BOM). The Microchip datasheet (DS25073A) gives that option an input noise of 950/G_MIN = **95 nV/rtHz**, about 100 times the thermal noise of a 50 ohm source. Their gain is 1 + R_F/R_G with R_G = R254/R266 = 1 k and R_F = the MAX5388 pot (100 k), so the gain runs from 1 to 101 and the default pot setting of 200 is about 79 (38 dB). The measurement agrees: stepping the pot from 20 to 255 raised the signal 11x and the noise 2.4x (135 to 326 in the meter's units). At low gain the floor is the ADC (about 135, half an ADC count); from the default setting upward it is the in-amp's own input noise being amplified. Working back from -85 dBm at the antenna through about 7 dB of mixer loss gives roughly 110 nV/rtHz at the in-amp input - the datasheet figure. So more gain in that stage buys only a couple of dB. Gain *before* it is what counts, which is why the HMC1023's 20 dB improves sensitivity by its whole amount.
  - **What would improve it, smallest change first:**
    1. *No hardware:* run receive through the HMC1023 at +20 dB (needs the VDDBG fix): about -108 dBm. Default the gain pot to 255 (about 2 dB).
    2. *Pin-compatible swap:* fit MCP6N11-**100** in place of the -010 (same package and pinout, 35 nV/rtHz, 35 MHz gain-bandwidth) and change R254/R266 from 1 k to 100 ohms so the gain range becomes about 10 to 1000; that option is only stable at a gain of 100 or more, so the firmware must keep the pot above about code 26. Expected about 9 dB lower noise in that stage: roughly -94 dBm in bypass, and perhaps -115 dBm through the chip if nothing else gets in the way. Not tried.
    3. *Next board revision:* a genuinely low-noise first baseband stage (1 to 3 nV/rtHz, e.g. a low-noise op-amp or in-amp, or a step-up transformer ahead of it) and/or an RF preamp ahead of the mixers. That is the route to -125 dBm and beyond; the limit then is mixer loss plus a few dB, around -130 dBm in an SSB bandwidth.
  - **For scale, band noise at a quiet rural site in an SSB bandwidth is very roughly** -95 to -100 dBm on 80 m, -105 dBm on 40 m, -115 dBm on 20 m, -125 dBm on 10 m and -135 dBm on 2 m (from memory of the ITU noise curves; treat as rough). So about -108 dBm is already band-noise-limited on 80 and 40 m, short by several dB on 20 m, and well short on 10 m and 2 m.
  - **FM broadcast: first version DONE 2026-11-10** - see the "Wideband FM" entry below.
  - The auto filter corner costs 0 to 6 dB of sensitivity against filter-off at several frequencies, so filter-off stays the receive default; a more generous rule (corner at least 2x the dial frequency) or the chip's calibration might remove that.
  - Frequency calibration of this unit against the LibreVNA: -71,995 ppb (stored with `J`).
- **Wideband FM (broadcast) receive: first version DONE 2026-11-10.** A fourth mode, "WFM" (mode menu, console `D3`), in `Source/src/wfm.c`. It works on the raw 720 kHz ADC samples: average by 3 to 240 kHz I/Q, phase-difference detector, average to 48 kHz, DC block, 75 us de-emphasis, mono, out through the same path as AM (speaker and USB). The gain pot is turned down to 80 in this mode. Checked with the LibreVNA at 100 MHz: the detector reads a carrier's offset to within 0.3 kHz, and tracks it out to +/-88 kHz at -40 dBm but only to about +/-55 kHz at -60 dBm. **Not yet listened to on a real antenna.**
- **Wideband FM, second pass, 2026-11-10** (after Michael listened: "every station is noisy, a lot of distortion").
  - **The distortion was the analog anti-aliasing filter.** Traced from the netlist + BOM: each in-amp output goes through a 100n/100k AC coupling (16 Hz), then a unity-gain Sallen-Key low-pass on a FAN4174 (I: R50/R55 = 10k, C51/C54 = 1n, U47; Q: R61/R66, C62/C65, U58), then another 100n/100k coupling to the ADC pin (PA3 = I, PA2 = Q). Two poles at 1/(2 pi 10k 1n) = 15.9 kHz (Q = 0.5), so 3 dB down at 10.2 kHz: -10.8 / -20.7 / -27.3 dB at 25 / 50 / 75 kHz, matching the bench figures (-10 / -19 / -25). C49/C60 are only the coupling capacitors. The "about 8 kHz" quoted earlier was a one-pole fit to those bench points. That filter is correct for the 48 kHz modes; it is just far too narrow for broadcast FM, and its phase shift (not its amplitude) is what distorts the audio: about 7 dB signal-to-distortion in simulation regardless of signal strength.
  - **Fix: digital equaliser** (inverse of the two poles) on the 240 kHz samples ahead of the detector, console `j<Hz> <Hz>`, default 15900 15900. Simulation: 37 dB or better on a strong signal. On the air (94.9 MHz) the amplitude wobble of the signal (`ripple` in `P`, 0 for a perfect FM signal) went 0.88 -> 0.25, and 15.9k/15.9k was the best of everything tried, which independently confirms the schematic values. It boosts far-off noise, so weak stations do not improve; a channel filter after the equaliser is the next thing to try for those.
  - Also: a 15 kHz 4th-order low-pass at 240 kHz before decimating (the stereo subcarrier was aliasing into the audio), baseband gain now starts at maximum and backs off near clipping, detector noise figure in `P`.
  - Other poles to remember: the MCP6N11-010 closed-loop bandwidth is about 5 MHz / gain, so roughly 63 kHz at pot 200 and 50 kHz at pot 255. Not equalised.
  - Hardware idea if broadcast FM matters: nothing to change for the HF modes, but 1n -> 100p on C51/C54/C62/C65 would move the filter to 159 kHz; aliasing protection would then rest on the 720 kHz sampling + 15x average. Not recommended without measuring.
  - **USB product ID changed 0x5740 -> 0x5750.** ST's Virtual COM Port driver (installed with ST's tools) matched the old ID and hid the two sound-card interfaces on this PC. Still a borrowed vendor ID.
- **Narrow FM receive: first version 2026-11-10, NOT yet tested on a real signal.** Fifth mode "NFM" (mode menu, console `D4`). Runs in the 48 kHz path: AM's DC blockers and channel filter fixed at 7.5 kHz, phase-difference detector, 300 Hz de-emphasis, 300 Hz high-pass (CTCSS), 3 kHz low-pass. Squelch from detector noise above 5.5 kHz: console `u<level>`, `u0` = open (the default), `u` reports; with no signal the noise reads about 4100, so something like `u3000` should close it. S-meter reads the channel power. To do: test with a handheld into a dummy load nearby or a signal generator with FM, pick a default squelch and put it on a menu item, transmit FM (modulating the phase of the I/Q output is straightforward), repeater offset + CTCSS encode.
  - **Finding: the analog baseband has a low-pass near 8 kHz** (10 dB down at 25 kHz, 19 dB at 50 kHz, 25 dB at 75 kHz), presumably the anti-alias network after the in-amps from when the firmware sampled at 20 kHz. That is why weak FM stations lose their deviation peaks, and it also means the 48 kHz USB I/Q output is only flat over the middle few kHz. Widening it (probably C49/C60 and their resistors - not traced) would make WFM work on weaker stations and give a usable +/-24 kHz panadapter; the oversampling ADC path no longer needs such a low corner.
  - To do: a "WFM" label bitmap, 100 kHz tuning steps, a proper channel filter ahead of the detector, stereo is out of reach without more bandwidth.
- **Transmit sideband trim: DONE 2026-11-10.** Console `z` holds a per-unit phase and gain correction for the transmit I/Q pair (plus DC offsets), and `tools/tx_trim_cal.py` finds the values with an RSA306. On the modified unit at 7.1 MHz the opposite sideband went from -33 dBc to about -55 dBc. The DC-offset half does nothing on this hardware: stepping it +/-100 counts did not move the carrier, so the DAC-to-mixer path is AC-coupled. The carrier is about -50 dBc as it is. To do: per-band values (the best phase/gain will drift with frequency).
- **Transmit noise comes from the DAC reference rail (found 2026-11-10) - hardware item.** With the transmitter on and no modulation at all, the radio put out broadband noise only about 79 dB/Hz below the carrier (signal-to-noise about 45 dB in an SSB bandwidth), on every path. It is not the receive in-amps or the mic preamp (switching them off changed nothing). It scales exactly with the DAC's resting code (-6 dB at half, -10 dB at 600/2048), so it is noise on the 3.3 V analog rail that the DACs use as their reference. Firmware now parks the DACs just above the signal's peak instead of at mid-scale, which lowers the noise between CW elements and speech by about 8 dB and by less at full drive. The real fix is hardware: filter VDDA/VREF+ (LC or RC) or give the DACs and ADCs a quiet reference. **Worth checking for receive too:** the ADCs use the same reference, and the receive DC offset is a few percent of full scale, so the same rail noise may be part of the receive noise floor. Put a scope / spectrum analyzer on VDDA.
- **12-bit DAC limits transmit level.** With the test tone, drive 25 is about half scale and drive 50 clips, which is what the old "driver compressing at drive 80" really was. The top steps are now drive 45. Through the HMC1023 that gives about +3 dBm with 3rd-order products at -23 dBc (yellow), so +0 dBm at drive 25 remains the clean setting.
- **Transmit frequency range (measured 2026-11-10, modified unit).** Output is flat from 3.4 MHz to 175 MHz, the firmware's upper tuning limit: about -21 dBm in bypass and 0 to +2 dBm through the HMC1023 at every frequency tried (3.4, 3.6, 7.1, 10.1, 14.2, 18.1, 21.3, 24.9, 28.5, 35, 45, 50.1, 60, 70, 80, 100, 120, 144.2, 160, 174, 175 MHz). Through the chip the 3rd harmonic is 48 to 65 dB down on all HF bands. Above 55 MHz no corner fits, so the chip's filter is bypassed and only its gain is used: harmonics are unfiltered there. **The dead zone at and below 3.2 MHz is FIXED:** it was a 32-bit overflow in the Si5338 divider arithmetic for LO frequencies of 6.4 MHz and lower (`writeMultiSynthParameters`), which hit receive as well. Transmit now works down to 2.5 MHz, the firmware's lower limit (the Si5338's outputs go down to 5 MHz and the LO is twice the dial). Going lower (160 m, the AM broadcast band) needs the Si5338's output R divider. Receive range was not measured (no signal source on the bench that night); it shares the LO, so the same lower limit is likely.
- **Both HMC parts together (HMC1023 +20 dB into the HMC482), measured 2026-11-10.** Maximum about +14.6 dBm (29 mW); the HMC482 saturates between +12 and +15 dBm whatever feeds it. Because the HMC482 comes after the filter, its own harmonics go straight out: 2nd -6 to -14 dBc, 3rd -8 to -15 dBc. Lines around 2.2-2.3 GHz at -11 to -24 dBm appeared at most settings. So even with the oscillation fixed, this amp tops out near 25 mW and needs a low-pass filter after it. More power needs a different final stage.
- **Automatic LPF corner: DONE 2026-11-10 (register-level).** With receive through the chip, corner setting `auto` (console `ka`, or the panel) picks the lowest of 5/7/10/14/20/28/40/50/72 MHz that is at least 1.3x the dial frequency and follows the dial; `k0`..`k8` fix it, `k9` turns the filter off (still the receive default). Verified by register read-back and on transmit. NOT yet verified on receive with real signals - earlier on-air notes found receive best with the filter off, so compare before making auto the default.
- **HMC482 IS UNSTABLE: oscillates at 2.547 GHz (found 2026-11-10) - hardware fix needed before the amp is used on the air.** With the amp powered, the unit measured breaks into a parasitic oscillation at about 2.547 GHz, +8 to +10 dBm at the antenna port (stronger than the wanted signal, 2nd harmonic at 5.09 GHz), and the 7 MHz gain drops 11-17 dB while it does (the amp is being saturated by its own oscillation). Whether it oscillates depends on drive AND load: into a 40 dB pad it happened at drive 12-16 only; into a 10 dB pad at drive 0-2 and 8-14 (yes, with no drive at all) and partly at 20. At the top amp settings there is a weaker line near 2.3-2.5 GHz, about -10 to -13 dBm. So no amp setting can be called clean. One unit, one band. It probably also explains why powering the amp raised the receive noise floor.
  - Things to try on the bench, watching 2-3 GHz on the analyzer (none tried yet): a few-pF to ~22 pF shunt capacitor at the amp input and/or output so it has no gain left at GHz (the radio only needs 35 MHz); a ferrite bead or 10-22 ohm resistor in series with the output; GHz-rated decoupling (100 pF) right at the supply side of the bias chokes L11/L19, which at 220 uH are far past self-resonance at GHz; check which part is really fitted (BOM says SXB-2089Z, footprint says HMC482) and its recommended bias for a 5 V supply.
  - Alternative to test on a board with the VDDBG fix: use the HMC1023 on transmit (mux channel 3) as both the harmonic low-pass filter and a +20 dB gain stage, and leave the HMC482 off for low power.
- **TX power setting: DONE 2026-11-10.** Menu item "TX" (console `w<0-4>`), five steps shown in dBm and mW from a per-unit calibration table (console `y<step> <dBm x10>`, stored in backup registers; `tools/tx_power_cal.py` fills it from an RSA306 and checks 2-3 GHz).
